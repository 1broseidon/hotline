# Live calls

The desk remains the primary call target. A conversation can also call its
teammate through the same session, chapter, harness and standing grants used
by a typed operator message. Hanging up, holding or interrupting speech does
not cancel accepted teammate work.

## Wire contract ledger

| Entry | Compatibility | Enforcement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `voice.call_start` | Optional `personaId` selects a teammate; omitted selects the desk. Optional `streamAudio` enables progressive sentence audio. | Owner/local desk only. Validate target before replacing an existing call. Echo the target; reject reuse with a different target. |
| `voiceDirectCalls` capability in remote hello and `voice.status.capabilities` | Additive; clients require it before sending a target. | A client must check the echoed target to avoid an older desk silently routing the call to its dispatcher. |
| `voiceListenWhileThinking` capability; `state.listening` | Additive; `listening: true` appears only on a direct call's `thinking` once the person's words are with the teammate, and is omitted otherwise. An older client ignores it and keeps the microphone shut while thinking. | The desk accepts an utterance whenever no earlier one is still being taken, so the flag only tells a client it may listen. A client listens through it unless speech is playing, keeps its blip-blip out of its detector, and does not play over the person while they talk. |
| `voiceFront` capability in remote hello and `voice.status.capabilities`; `voice.call_start.mode: "front"`, `inputRate`; reply `front: {inputRate, outputRate}` | Additive. A client asks with `mode: "front"` and treats the reply as the answer: `front` present means the call has a voice front, absent means the pipeline, which is what an older desk and a desk with no realtime provider both say, and what a new client does with them. `inputRate` is 16000 or 24000 (else the front's own 24000); the desk resamples the 16 kHz microphone up. `outputRate` is 24000. | Owner/local desk only, as the rest of `voice.*`; a phone seat is denied. The front is started only for a request that names it, and a retained call cannot drop its front by asking for the pipeline. While the call has a front, `voice.utterance`, `voice.text` and `voice.audio` are refused; once the front ends they work again. |
| `voice.front_audio` `{callId,seq,data}` | Microphone PCM16 little endian, mono, at the negotiated `inputRate`, at most 32 KiB, base64. `seq` rises (gaps are allowed: a dropped frame is just lost audio). Chunks of about 20-100 ms are the intent. | Never waits: a front still connecting, or whose bounded queue is full, refuses the frame with an error and the client drops it and sends the next. A held call refuses audio. |
| `frontAudio` `{seq,data}` event | The front's speech as PCM16 mono at `outputRate`, in frames of at most 100 ms. `seq` rises by one from 0, so a gap tells the client the desk lost frames. | Only a front call emits it; held calls emit none. A client too slow to read the call's event queue is disconnected, as for clips. |
| `voice.front_barge` `{callId,playedMs}`, `frontStop` event | Barge-in. When the provider hears the person over the front, the desk sends `frontStop`: the client stops and clears playback, then answers with `voice.front_barge` and the milliseconds actually played. A client that detects speech first may send `voice.front_barge` at once. The front keeps only `playedMs` of the interrupted reply. | Owner/local desk. The call returns to `listening`; the teammate's work is not touched. |
| `frontEnded` event | The front is gone for the rest of the call (it could not open, failed, or its session ended). The call goes on as a pipeline call; the client resumes sending utterances or text. | The call is not ended and its budget, seat and lease are unchanged. |
| Front call states | `listening` while the front waits, `speaking` while it has audio going out, `thinking` with `listening: true` while the teammate works (from the moment the front's `send_to_teammate` is accepted until the turn ends, as on a pipeline call). `heard {seq,text}` carries what the person said, as the front heard it, and `said {id,text}` what the front said, once its reply is whole, under an id starting `front-`; the teammate's own spoken lines still arrive as `said` under their reply's id, and the desk synthesizes no clips for them: the front says them. | Same `state` events as a pipeline call; an older client sees no new states. |
| `VoiceStatus.directAvailable` | Additive; older desks omit it, so clients fall back to `available`. Desk `available` still includes dispatcher readiness. | Direct readiness requires speech and budget, independent of the dispatcher. Owner/local desk seat enforcement is unchanged. |
| `VoiceStatus.replies` | Additive and read-only; absent until a reply has been counted, and an older client ignores it. One entry per model (`hotline/<provider>/<model>`, `acp/<adapter>[/<model>]`) counting the replies a direct call said as `both`, `spokenOnly`, `unclosed`, `untagged` or `rewritten`. | Counted once per reply that reached its end, in `voice-replies.json` under the data directory, written atomically; each count logs one `[voice]` line. One short line with no spoken version, said as written, is not counted. |
| `voiceTextInput`, `voice.status.inputMode`, `voice.call_start.inputMode` | Additive; omission retains audio. A text call echoes `inputMode: "text"` and accepts `text/plain`. | Output-only readiness needs no STT configuration. A retained call ID cannot switch target or input mode. |
| `voice.text` | One finalized device transcript `{callId,seq,text}` per turn on a negotiated text call. | Owner/local desk, opening connection, increasing sequence, pending-turn gate and cancellation remain enforced. Nonblank, at most 8,000 characters/32,000 UTF-8 bytes. No remote STT request or spend. |
| `voice.audio` | Negotiated by `audio/pcm` in the call's input list. Existing WAV/AAC utterances remain available. | PCM16 little endian, mono, 16 kHz. At most 32 KiB per chunk and 20 seconds per turn. Sequence and chunk index increase. Empty final chunk commits. No partial transcript can dispatch work. |
| `clip` events | Existing independently playable WAV/MP3 clips, increasing indices; last chunk has `final: true`. Native xAI starts with a 200 ms clip, then keeps half-second chunks and the final tail. | Bounded producer channel; cancellation drops provider work and rejects stale output. Whole-clip fallback for other providers/clients. |
| Direct replies | The teammate's own reply, its `<spoken>` version said as it streams: the first message of a turn as it streams, a report when it lands; narration between tools is not said. Text before a spoken version is held, and said just before it as a reply with no tags is. Without a spoken version, nothing is said while the reply streams; once it is whole, one short line is said as written, and anything longer is said as the call assistant's rewrite (stored as the reply's `spoken`, counted `rewritten`), or, with no call assistant, a refused Chat budget, a failure or no answer in 6 s, as its opening up to the first code block or table, at most three sentences (counted `untagged`). The call is `thinking` while the rewrite is written. Said text is cleaned for speech. One message is one `said` id and one thread line, closed by an empty final clip. No `delivery` event: the teammate is the voice. | Internal call/turn origin follows queued or accepted steering inputs. Unrelated agent output cannot enter a direct call. A reply to any turn of this call up to the latest is accepted. A partial tag at a chunk edge is held back and never said. |
| Direct call record | A direct call is a thread: its person and voice lines are written to `calls/<callId>.jsonl` behind the call, and the DM carries one `call` marker (`id`, `ts`, `callId`, `title`, `status` `live` or `ended`, `durationMs`, `outcome`) rewritten as the call goes. | Additive; a client that does not know the kind skips it (the phone does). A write never waits on the call's path to the ear. Desk calls stay on the dispatcher tape and have no marker. |
| Direct turns | Voice front ([ADR 0004](adr/0004-live-calls-get-a-voice-front.md)), by default (Settings › Use for › Live calls picks Grok Voice unless GPT-Live, GPT Realtime or Legacy voice is chosen) whenever the client asks for `mode: "front"`, which the window does for desk and teammate calls alike: a realtime voice front hears and speaks on the call and relays to the teammate, which does the work in its own session. The front relays and clarifies and never claims work; the client picks the spoken text. That relay is what runs today: [ADR 0005](adr/0005-front-is-a-full-agent.md) makes the front a full agent on a direct call, answering itself, running the teammate's desk-side tools on all three fronts and handing the session only heavy work, with the chat getting call summaries instead of per-turn lines, and this row is rewritten as each part of epic-7 lands. On a direct call the front also holds the teammate's desk-side reads (`voice/front_tools.rs`): `search_thread`, `list_chapters`, `list_schedules`, `web_search` and the tools of the MCP servers its policy grants, run on the desk through the same handler as a typed turn, under the teammate's current capability lease (a grant changed on the card refuses the next call), each output clipped to 4,000 characters before the model hears it and nothing posted to the DM; the front's own turns are never posted either: the call's thread keeps them, and the DM gets one summary from the teammate, in the first person and tagged "From the call:" (what was asked and what the front answered, that it looked things up, work still being done, a question left open; no tool names), posted as an agent message at hang-up and every 5 minutes on a longer call, scrubbed and clipped as `front_prompt.rs` does, plus whatever the person asks the front to put in the chat with `post_to_chat(text)` (an agent message tagged the same way, under the same lease check as the other tools). The summary is the front's own words from the call, composed in `voice/record.rs`; a live session also gets it ahead of its next message, and a session that starts later reads it from the tape, so a typed follow-up carries on from the call; a tool runs beside the call and the front speaks when the result is in. A desk call holds none. Grok Voice and GPT Realtime take them as session tools; GPT-Live takes them as the functions of a `responses` delegation on a backend model (`BACKEND_MODEL` in `realtime/live.rs`), and each function call the backend makes returns to the desk as a `response.event` and is answered with `function_call_output` and a `response.create`. A desk call keeps client delegation. Whether the ChatGPT sign-in accepts `responses` delegation is unverified. The bridge (`voice/front.rs`) gives the front two tools of its own, `send_to_teammate(words)` and `remain_silent`: the words go in unrephrased by the call's text input, so the same session, grants and chapter as a typed message, a second send steers the running turn, and the teammate's turn carries a note that the input is a transcript behind an intermediary (each relayed turn says so; there is no end line, because a turn without it is the person typing). Back to the front go the reply's spoken lines verbatim, a progress line while the turn runs, and a line that the person is needed on the desk or phone for a pending card, each given to the front as a text item to say word for word; a progress line after 45 s, then 90 s, then every 3 minutes, taken in turn from a short set so it does not repeat. A send asks the front for no reply: it acknowledges a request once, as it sends it, and stays quiet until the result comes back; a relay that echoes a line it was given is refused, so a front can never loop the teammate's reply back to it. On a direct call the front is the teammate, in the first person: it acknowledges a request as a colleague would ("One sec, I'll pull that up"), its fixed lines say "I", and it never says it passed something on or is waiting on someone. On a desk call it speaks for the desk and says it is passing the request on. `remain_silent` is answered without asking the front for a reply. The front opens with `front_prompt.rs`: the teammate's name, the last chapter's handoff note and the latest DM lines said in words, scrubbed. The front's own speech never reaches the teammate's tape; the call's thread keeps what the front heard and said, and the call card's transcript key shows it during the call (both sides, including what the front answered on its own), while the conversation tags the person's relayed lines "Said on the call". A call that ends on the person's question with nothing answering it (neither relayed nor replied to in full by the front) hands that question to the teammate after the call, as a line from the call with `Origin::ended` set: the teammate is told the call is over (`spoken::ENDED_NOTE`) and answers in writing, with no spoken version asked for. Only a question or a request of four words or more follows up, never a goodbye. A paid front needs room in the Voice budget to open, is charged each minute and when it stops, and ends the call when the budget is spent; revoking its login drops the front and the call goes on as a pipeline call. The pipeline below is the fallback. With no front: every utterance is a turn of the teammate's own session, in its open chapter, steering a turn still running. The driver is handed the words and then the contract asking for the answer twice, `<spoken>` then `<written>`; the tape and chat keep the words alone. The chat shows the written version; the reply's first agent event carries the spoken version as optional `spoken`, which an older client ignores. A history rebuilt from the tape shows the model both versions, tagged. The call is `thinking` until the turn ends or parks on subagents, re-sent every 15 s, with `listening: true` once the words are handed over, so the person can speak without a tap; speaking then, or `voice.interrupt`, stops what the call is saying without stopping the turn. Every agent message, on a call or not, is shown, written and pushed as its written version, without a tag. The call assistant answers desk calls; on a direct call it only rewrites a reply with no spoken version. | The contract is turn text, not a setting, so Hotline Agent and ACP agents get it alike. Only a direct call's origin adds it; a desk call's handoff and typed input never carry it. A direct call names no call-assistant spend up front; a rewrite reserves on Chat when asked for, and a refusal falls back without ending the call. |

Changes to optional fields are additive (R1); routing, streaming and reply
attribution change behavior (R2). Core owns origin and seat enforcement;
clients cannot claim voice origin through a normal session command.

Native xAI API keys support live PCM transcription and progressive PCM speech.
Other providers retain their existing whole-clip adapters. The separate explicit
`xai-subscription` speech choice uses the stored Grok login, resolves fresh OAuth
credentials per request, and retries once after a rejected bearer. It is never
selected automatically and cannot use a paid speech fallback. Provider-side
subscription limits remain applicable.

Progressive xAI output publishes the first 200 ms once one further PCM16 sample
is buffered. It no longer waits for a full second of audio before publishing.
The retained sample keeps the final clip nonempty when the response ends at the
first boundary; shorter responses still publish one whole final clip. Later
chunks retain a half-second tail. This changes clip duration (R2), with the same
WAV format, ordering, final marker, budget and cancellation contracts.

Server logs measure accepted utterance to first audio publication. Client
timing measures the end of capture to playback start. Provider and device
latency still require live measurement; tests establish ordering and
cancellation rather than claiming a particular latency reduction.

## Verification and rollout

Headless wire/session tests cover desk compatibility, target validation,
owner/companion boundaries, stream bounds, commit/cancellation and reply
isolation, plus output-only text configuration, sequence/mode/bounds and no STT
spend. Provider protocol tests use local fixtures. UI and mobile tests
cover negotiated fallback, target pinning and ordered playback.

Release the core/server and desktop first. Mobile uses capabilities and native
speech-recognition/AudioStream feature checks; older desks and installed runtimes retain
the existing AAC call path. An OTA must not assume a newly installed native
module. Device audio routing and real provider performance need an iPhone
call after rollout.
