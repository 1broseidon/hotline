description: |-
  When a teammate ends a reply on a real choice, it can offer 2–3 short answers as chips above the composer. One tap sends that answer as your message. Most replies offer none, so talking to a teammate never feels like a quiz. People still just chat, and the chips are a shortcut.

  Design approved 2026-09-26: concept A, "Suggested replies", in the composer mockup attached to BRO-136.

  ## The agent decides

  * Add a tool, e.g. `offer_replies(["Publish now", "Hold for tonight"])`. The chips come only from this call and are never guessed from the reply text. No call means no chips.
  * Tell the agent when to use it, in the tool's description and the system guidance:
    * Only when the reply is waiting on the person for a choice with 2–3 clear answers, such as approve or hold, or option A or B.
    * Never after it has reported, finished or explained something.
    * Never on two replies in a row.
    * Each answer is at most about 24 characters and reads as the person's own words.
  * The offer belongs to the reply it came with. It's stored on the tape with that reply, so desk and phone show the same thing.

  ## The composer

  * Chips sit in a row above the capsule. The capsule stays 44pt and nothing takes its place.
  * Tapping a chip sends its text as an ordinary message, the same as typing it.
  * The chips go away as soon as you type, or when any message arrives after the reply.
  * This works on desktop and mobile.

  ## Out of scope

  Chips generated by the client, and follow-up suggestions after an answer.
